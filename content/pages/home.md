## Why I do this

In our culture, across the board, we have fears of death. We have fears because
we don’t know what is coming next. We have fears that we haven’t done enough in
our lives. We have regrets. We have things we want to tell our children and we
don’t know how.

And when you are dying, the people closest to you are already grieving. They are
already dealing with everything it is bringing up in them. So you are careful.
You don’t want to burden them with any more than they are already carrying.

Which means the person who is dying very often ends up with nobody to process
their own grief, their own fears, their own regrets with.

I want to be that person.

> I want to provide the opportunity for people to be really held and heard — in
> their story, in their fears, in their joys, in their regrets, in the legacy
> they want to leave behind. I just don’t think there is enough of that in our
> culture.

We are more socially isolated than we have ever been, and that is felt most
keenly in the last stages of a life.

Some people are already surrounded. They have family who will not shy away from
the hard conversation; some of them are even looking forward to dying, and say
so. I am glad for them, and I am not here for them. I am here for the person who
has nobody they can say the frightening thing to.
