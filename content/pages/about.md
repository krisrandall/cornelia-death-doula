## What a death doula actually is

A death doula keeps company with a person who is dying, and with the people
around them. Not as a nurse and not as a therapist — as a person who turns up,
stays, and can hear anything you need to say.

When I first came to this work I thought I had to make it more complicated than
that. I don’t.

> I am just creating a relationship with a person who is dying, and holding
> space for them. That’s it.

## My training

I trained as a death doula through Denise Love, at a course in Hervey Bay
earlier this year, and I hold a death doula certificate.

The certificate matters, and it is also not really the thing. What I bring is
that I am genuinely capable of having hard conversations, and of not looking
away from them. That is the part that took a life to learn.

## Why it is mostly about who is *not* there

I am fortunate. I have a lot of people in my life who will not shy away from the
conversation. Most people do not have that, and it becomes very clear at the
end.

If you are dying, your nearest and dearest are already somewhere in their own
grief. You feel — rightly or not — that you have to be careful with them. So the
fears and the regrets and the unsaid things stay in the room with you.

I am the person you do not have to be careful with.

## What I am not

I am not a doctor or a nurse, and I do not give medical or nursing care. I am
not a counsellor, a psychologist or a chaplain. I am not a funeral director. I
do not replace palliative care, and I don’t compete with it — I work alongside
it, doing the part nobody has time to do.

I also don’t claim to know what happens next. If you want to talk about that, I
will talk about it all night. I just won’t pretend to have the answer.
